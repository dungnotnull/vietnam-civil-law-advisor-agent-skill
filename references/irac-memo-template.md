# IRAC Memo Template

Standard template for producing legal memos using the IRAC (Issue-Rule-Application-Conclusion) framework for Vietnamese civil law matters. This template operationalizes systematic legal analysis consistent with civil-law methodology.

## Template Structure

```markdown
# LEGAL MEMO

**TO**: [Recipient Name/Title]
**FROM**: [Attorney/Analyst Name]
**DATE**: [Current Date]
**RE**: [Subject Matter]
**REFERENCE**: [Relevant Civil Code 2015 Provisions]

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

[Brief 1-2 paragraph summary of the legal question, analysis, and conclusion. The executive summary should allow a busy reader to understand the key findings without reading the full memo.]

---

## DISCLAIMER

> This memo provides general, educational/analytical information only. It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified legal professional. Outputs are analytical tools to support, not replace, professional legal judgment. Always verify with a qualified Vietnamese lawyer before making decisions or taking action based on this memo.

---

## ISSUE

[Clear, concise statement of the legal question to be analyzed. The issue should be specific and framed as a question that can be answered through application of legal rules to facts.]

**Format Examples:**
- Single issue: "Whether [legal question]"
- Multiple issues: "Whether [legal question 1]; whether [legal question 2]"

**Example:**
"Whether a contract clause specifying liquidated damages at 20% of the contract value is enforceable under Vietnamese law when the actual damage from breach is estimated at 12% of contract value."

---

## RULE

[Statement of applicable legal rules, including Civil Code 2015 articles and relevant precedent. This section should include:]

### Relevant Civil Code 2015 Provisions

**Primary Statutes:**
- **Article [Number]**: [Title/Description]
  - [Key text of article, focusing on elements relevant to the issue]
  - [Additional relevant text if needed]

**Example:**
- **Article 363**: Agreed penalty
  - "Where a party breaches an obligation under a contract, the parties may agree in advance that the breaching party shall pay an agreed penalty to the non-breaching party, except where otherwise agreed by the parties..."
  - "The agreed penalty may be increased or reduced by a court if such penalty is obviously too high or too low compared to the damage actually caused..."

### Supporting Legal Provisions

[Include other relevant Civil Code articles or supporting laws]

**Example:**
- **Article 360**: Compensation for damage
  - "Where damage caused by a party which does not perform or does not properly perform an obligation under a contract, that party must compensate..."
- **Article 117**: Conditions for civil transactions to be valid
  - "A civil transaction is valid when all of the following conditions are met: Parties have full civil act capacity; Parties participate voluntarily..."

### Relevant Precedent

[Include relevant Supreme Court precedent, if any]

**Example:**
- **Precedent No. 04/2016/AL**: Agreed Penalty
  - **Issue**: Enforceability of agreed penalty substantially exceeding actual damage
  - **Holding**: Courts may reduce agreed penalty if it substantially exceeds actual damage and constitutes unjust enrichment
  - **Reasoning**: The purpose of agreed penalty is to compensate damage, not to create windfall; excessive penalties violate principle of fairness

### Legal Principles

[State applicable legal principles derived from statutes and precedent]

**Example:**
1. **Principle of Fairness**: Agreed penalties should compensate actual damage, not create unjust enrichment
2. **Freedom of Contract**: Parties may agree on penalties subject to legal limits
3. **Court Authority**: Courts have authority to modify excessive penalties

---

## APPLICATION

[Application of legal rules to specific facts. This section should systematically apply each element of the legal rules to the facts.]

### Element 1: [First Legal Element]

**Legal Requirement**: [State what the law requires for this element]

**Facts**: [State the relevant facts]

**Analysis**: [Apply the legal requirement to the facts]

**Example:**
**Legal Requirement**: Under Article 117, CC2015, a contract clause is valid when parties have full civil act capacity, participate voluntarily, and the clause's purpose and content do not violate law.

**Facts**: The parties to the contract are Company A (a properly registered legal entity) and Company B (also properly registered). The contract was executed after arm's length negotiation over 30 days. Both companies signed voluntarily through authorized representatives.

**Analysis**: Both parties meet the capacity requirements (properly registered legal entities with authorized representatives). The voluntary participation requirement is satisfied (30-day negotiation period, arm's length negotiation). The clause's purpose (providing for damages in case of breach) is lawful and not prohibited by law.

**Conclusion on Element 1**: The validity requirements of Article 117 are satisfied.

### Element 2: [Second Legal Element]

**Legal Requirement**: [State what the law requires for this element]

**Facts**: [State the relevant facts]

**Analysis**: [Apply the legal requirement to the facts]

**Example:**
**Legal Requirement**: Under Article 363, CC2015, agreed penalties may be reduced by courts if "obviously too high" compared to actual damage caused.

**Facts**: The contract specifies liquidated damages at 20% of contract value (total contract value: $1,000,000; liquidated damages: $200,000). Potential breach is failure to deliver goods on time. Actual damage from delayed delivery includes:
- Storage costs: $50,000
- Alternative procurement costs: $40,000
- Lost profit: $30,000
- Total actual damage: $120,000 (12% of contract value)

**Analysis**: The agreed penalty ($200,000) is 67% higher than actual damage ($120,000). Under Precedent No. 04/2016/AL, such a substantial difference suggests the penalty is "obviously too high" and may constitute unjust enrichment. The purpose of liquidated damages is to compensate damage, not provide windfall.

**Conclusion on Element 2**: The 20% liquidated damages clause may be considered "obviously too high" and subject to reduction.

### Synthesis

[Combine analysis of all elements to reach overall conclusion]

**Example:**
Combining the analysis: The contract clause satisfies all validity requirements under Article 117, but the penalty amount under Article 363 may be reduced. The clause is valid as executed, but the penalty amount may be modified by court to align with actual damage.

---

## CONCLUSION

[Clear, concise answer to the legal question stated in the Issue section. The conclusion should flow directly from the Application section.]

**Format**: Direct answer to the question stated in Issue, with reasoning summary.

**Example:**
The liquidated damages clause specifying 20% of contract value is valid under Article 117, CC2015, as it meets all validity requirements. However, under Article 363, CC2015, and Precedent No. 04/2016/AL, a court may reduce the penalty to approximately 12-15% of contract value to align with actual damage and avoid unjust enrichment. The clause is enforceable but subject to potential reduction.

---

## RECOMMENDATIONS

[Practical recommendations based on the legal analysis]

**Example:**
1. **For Contract Negotiation**: Consider setting liquidated damages closer to estimated actual damage (12-15% range) to avoid potential reduction.
2. **For Current Dispute**: If dispute exists, be prepared to argue for specific calculation method; consider settlement near 12-15% range.
3. **For Future Contracts**: Include specific damage calculation methodology to reduce uncertainty.
4. **Documentation Requirement**: Maintain detailed records of actual damages to support penalty amount if challenged.

---

## PRECEDENT CONSIDERATIONS

[Analysis of relevant precedent and how it applies]

### Relevant Supreme Court Precedent

**Precedent No. 04/2016/AL**: Agreed Penalty
- **Issue**: Reduction of agreed penalty exceeding actual damage
- **Holding**: Courts may reduce agreed penalty when substantially exceeding actual damage
- **Application**: Current case involves penalty 67% higher than actual damage, directly analogous to precedent
- **Weight**: Strong persuasive authority; Supreme Court precedent on same issue

### Gaps in Precedent

[Note if any aspects of the issue are not addressed by precedent]

**Example**:
No precedent directly addresses what percentage difference constitutes "obviously too high." Current precedent suggests substantial differences (like 67%) justify reduction, but does not establish specific threshold.

---

## ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS

[Any additional legal or practical considerations relevant to the issue]

**Example:**
- **Enforcement Practicality**: Even if reduced, penalty provides meaningful remedy
- **Business Relationship**: Consider impact of dispute on ongoing business relationship
- **Alternative Remedies**: Other remedies (specific performance, damages) also available
- **Contractual Context**: Penalty is one provision among many; consider overall contract balance

---

## FOOTNOTES (if applicable)

[Detailed citations, explanations, or additional analysis]

**Example:**
1. Article 363, Civil Code 2015 provides for agreed penalties in contracts and court authority to modify excessive penalties.
2. Precedent No. 04/2016/AL specifically addresses agreed penalties and provides framework for reduction analysis.
3. Article 117, Civil Code 2015 sets forth general validity requirements for civil transactions, including contract clauses.

---

END OF MEMO
```

## Template Application Instructions

### When to Use This Template

Use this IRAC memo template for:
- Formal legal opinion documents
- Client advice memoranda
- Internal legal analysis memoranda
- Case analysis and strategy documents
- Legal research summaries

### Systematic Application Process

**Step 1: Issue Formulation**
- Identify the specific legal question
- Frame question in "whether" format
- Ensure question is answerable through legal analysis

**Step 2: Rule Identification**
- Locate relevant Civil Code 2015 articles
- Identify applicable precedent
- Extract legal principles

**Step 3: Fact Gathering**
- Identify all relevant facts
- Organize facts by legal element
- Ensure factual completeness

**Step 4: Application**
- Apply each legal element systematically
- Connect rules to facts explicitly
- Use code-based syllogistic reasoning

**Step 5: Conclusion Formulation**
- Derive clear answer to issue
- Ensure conclusion flows from application
- Provide reasoning summary

**Step 6: Recommendation Development**
- Develop practical recommendations
- Ensure recommendations flow from analysis
- Consider client/practical implications

### Quality Checklist

Before finalizing memo, verify:

**Structural Elements:**
- [ ] Executive summary complete
- [ ] Disclaimer included
- [ ] Issue clearly stated
- [ ] Rule section comprehensive
- [ ] Application systematic
- [ ] Conclusion clear and supported
- [ ] Recommendations practical
- [ ] Precedent considerations addressed

**Legal Analysis:**
- [ ] All relevant Civil Code articles cited
- [ ] Relevant precedent included
- [ ] Application to facts explicit
- [ ] Reasoning sound and logical
- [ ] Conclusion follows from application

**Writing Quality:**
- [ ] Clear, professional language
- [ ] Proper legal citation format
- [ ] Logical organization
- [ ] Appropriate level of detail
- [ ] Free of grammatical errors

### Domain-Specific Adaptations

**Contract Law Memos:**
- Focus on validity, performance, breach, remedies
- Include contract interpretation analysis
- Address practical enforcement considerations

**Property Law Memos:**
- Include dual citation (CC2015 + LL2013)
- Address registration and formality issues
- Consider foreign entity restrictions if applicable

**Family Law Memos:**
- Include dual citation (CC2015 + MFL2014)
- Address emotional/relationship considerations
- Focus on best interests of children in custody matters

**Tort Liability Memos:**
- Structure around Article 584 elements
- Include damage analysis
- Address fault and causation explicitly

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Issue Section:**
- Don't combine multiple distinct issues
- Don't frame issue as a conclusion
- Don't use vague or overly broad language

**Rule Section:**
- Don't include irrelevant legal provisions
- Don't misstate or misquote articles
- Don't overlook relevant precedent

**Application Section:**
- Don't make conclusory statements without analysis
- Don't skip elements of legal test
- Don't ignore unfavorable facts

**Conclusion Section:**
- Don't introduce new analysis
- Don't reach conclusion unsupported by application
- Don't be vague or hedging

### Tips for Effective Memos

**Executive Summary:**
- Write last (after full analysis complete)
- Keep concise (1-2 paragraphs maximum)
- Include key question, answer, and implications

**Issue Section:**
- Frame as single question if possible
- Use precise legal terminology
- Ensure issue is narrow enough to answer

**Rule Section:**
- Organize logically (statutes first, then precedent)
- Quote key provisions accurately
- Explain legal concepts clearly

**Application Section:**
- Use separate headings for each element
- Apply facts systematically to each element
- Make reasoning explicit and clear

**Conclusion Section:**
- Answer question directly
- Provide reasoning summary
- Keep concise (one paragraph typically)

**Recommendations Section:**
- Make recommendations practical
- Ensure recommendations flow from analysis
- Prioritize by importance/actionability

## Integration with Skill Methodologies

This IRAC memo template operationalizes:

**1. Code-Based Syllogistic Reasoning:**
- Major premise: Legal rules from Civil Code
- Minor premise: Relevant facts
- Conclusion: Application of law to facts

**2. IRAC Framework:**
- Structure follows Issue-Rule-Application-Conclusion
- Each section builds systematically on previous sections
- Conclusion flows logically from application

**3. Civil-Law Methodology:**
- Primary reliance on Civil Code provisions
- Supplementary use of precedent
- Systematic application of legal rules

**4. Comparative Civil-Law Tradition:**
- Recognizes place within civil-law tradition
- Code-centered approach with precedent supplement
- Systematic legal reasoning

By using this template consistently, practitioners ensure comprehensive, systematic legal analysis that adheres to Vietnamese civil-law methodology and produces clear, actionable legal advice.
