# Contract Law Checklist

Comprehensive issue-spotting and validation checklist for contracts and civil transactions under Vietnamese Civil Code 2015. Use this checklist when analyzing contract validity, performance, breach, or remedies.

## Checklist Overview

This checklist operationalizes the civil-law code-based syllogistic reasoning methodology for contract analysis. Each item represents a legal requirement derived from specific Civil Code 2015 articles.

## Part 1: Contract Formation and Validity

### 1.1 Parties to the Contract

**Civil Code References:**
- Article 17: Civil act capacity
- Article 21: Conditions for validity of civil transactions

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Identity Verification**: Are parties clearly identified and capable of entering contracts?
  - Natural persons: Full legal capacity (Article 21)
  - Legal persons: Proper registration and authorization (Article 75)
  - Foreign entities: Proper legal standing in Vietnam

- [ ] **Authority Verification**: Does each party have authority to enter the contract?
  - Individual acting in personal capacity: Verified
  - Representative acting for legal person: Proper authorization (Article 40)
  - Agent acting on behalf: Valid power of attorney (Article 38)

- [ ] **Capacity Verification**: Do parties have full civil act capacity?
  - Adults (18+): Full capacity (Article 17)
  - Minors (15-18): Limited capacity - requires guardian consent (Article 21)
  - Persons with mental illness: No capacity - requires guardian (Article 22)

**Common Issues:**
- Parties lacking legal capacity
- Representatives without proper authorization
- Minors entering contracts without guardian consent
- Foreign entities without legal standing in Vietnam

### 1.2 Form and Formalities

**Civil Code References:**
- Articles 16-20: Form of civil transactions

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Written Form**: Does contract type require written form?
  - Real estate transactions: Written + notarization (Article 119)
  - Long-term contracts: Written form recommended
  - Simple transactions: Verbal may be sufficient

- [ ] **Notarization**: Is notarization required?
  - Real estate sale/purchase: Required (Land Law 2013)
  - Certain loan agreements: Required for amounts above threshold
  - Gifts of immovable property: Required

- [ ] **Registration**: Is registration required for effectiveness?
  - Real estate rights: Land registration required
  - Security interests: Mortgage registration required
  - Certain business contracts: Tax registration

- [ ] **Formal Language**: Is contract in required language?
  - Vietnamese contracts: Must be in Vietnamese
  - Foreign language contracts: Must have Vietnamese translation
  - Bilingual contracts: Vietnamese version controls

**Common Issues:**
- Missing required notarization
- Failure to register required transactions
- Contracts in foreign language without Vietnamese translation
- Oral agreements where written form required

### 1.3 Content and Legality

**Civil Code References:**
- Article 23: Transactions with contents violating law
- Article 24: Transactions with illegal purposes

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Legal Purpose**: Is contract purpose lawful?
  - Purpose not prohibited by law
  - Purpose not contrary to social morality
  - Purpose achievable under current law

- [ ] **Lawful Content**: Are all contract terms lawful?
  - No terms prohibited by law
  - No terms contravening mandatory provisions
  - No terms violating public policy

- [ ] **Specific Terms**: Are essential terms clearly defined?
  - Subject matter clearly identified
  - Price/payment terms clearly specified
  - Performance timeline established
  - Rights and obligations of parties defined

- [ ] **Compliance Checks**: Does contract comply with specific regulations?
  - Industry-specific regulations met
  - Foreign investment regulations (if applicable)
  - Tax and regulatory requirements addressed

**Common Issues:**
- Contracts with illegal purposes (e.g., gambling, illegal activities)
- Terms violating mandatory legal provisions
- Unclear or ambiguous essential terms
- Non-compliance with industry-specific regulations

### 2.2 Risk Allocation

**Civil Code References:**
- Articles 295-302: Risk in contract performance

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Force Majeure**: Are force majeure events defined?
  - Definition of force majeure provided
  - Notice requirements specified
  - Consequences of force majeure addressed
  - Recovery procedures defined

- [ ] **Hardship**: Is hardship provision included?
  - Hardship definition provided
  - Renegotiation mechanism specified
  - Termination provisions if renegotiation fails

- [ ] **Insurance**: Are insurance requirements specified?
  - Required insurance types identified
  - Insurance responsibilities allocated
  - Coverage requirements defined
  - Proof of insurance requirements

**Common Issues:**
- Missing force majeure provisions
- No hardship clause for changing circumstances
- Unclear insurance obligations
- Inadequate risk allocation

## Part 3: Breach and Remedies

### 3.1 Breach Identification

**Civil Code References:**
- Articles 355-369: Breach of contract

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Breach Types**: What type of breach occurred?
  - Non-performance: Failure to perform obligations
  - Delayed performance: Performance after deadline
  - Defective performance: Performance doesn't meet standards
  - Partial performance: Incomplete performance

- [ ] **Materiality**: Is breach material or minor?
  - Material breach: Goes to root of contract
  - Minor breach: Technical violation
  - Effect on contract purpose considered
  - Impact on other party evaluated

- [ ] **Causation**: Is breach clearly linked to damage?
  - Direct causation established
  - Damage foreseeability assessed
  - Intervening causes identified

**Common Issues:**
- Unclear breach classification
- Disputed materiality of breach
- Complex causation chains
- Intervening causes complicating liability

### 3.2 Remedy Analysis

**Civil Code References:**
- Articles 360-369: Remedies for breach

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Specific Performance**: Is specific performance available?
  - Performance still possible
  - Specific performance not excessively burdensome
  - No adequate substitute available
  - Monetary damages insufficient

- [ ] **Damages**: Are damages available and measurable?
  - Actual damages quantified
  - Consequential damages considered
  - Punitive damages (if available) assessed
  - Damage mitigation by injured party evaluated

- [ ] **Termination**: Is termination justified?
  - Material breach established
  - Cure period offered/considered
  - Termination notice given
  - Post-termination rights clarified

- [ ] **Price Reduction**: Is price reduction applicable?
  - Partial performance delivered
  - Performance value reduced
  - Proportional reduction calculated
  - Partial acceptance considered

**Common Issues:**
- Disputed damage calculations
- Unclear termination procedures
- Inadequate cure periods
- Complex mitigation analyses

## Part 6: Contract Review Template Application

### 6.1 Issue-Spotting Framework

**Using IRAC Methodology:**

For each identified issue:

1. **Issue**: State the legal question clearly
2. **Rule**: Cite relevant Civil Code articles
3. **Application**: Apply articles to specific contract terms
4. **Conclusion**: Derive legal conclusion

**Example:**
```
Issue: Whether the contract clause on liquidated damages is enforceable under Vietnamese law.

Rule: Article 363, Civil Code 2015 provides that liquidated damages may be agreed upon but must not exceed the actual damage suffered. Article 360 allows for damages arising from breach of contract.

Application: The contract clause specifies liquidated damages of 20% of contract value. The actual damage from the potential breach is estimated at 15% of contract value. The liquidated damages exceed the actual damage.

Conclusion: The liquidated damages clause may be reduced to 15% under Article 363, as courts may reduce excessive liquidated damages that do not reflect actual damage.
```

## Application Instructions

When using this checklist for contract analysis:

1. **Systematic Application**: Review each checklist item in order

3. **Legal Research**: Consult Civil Code 2015 articles for each flagged item

5. **Documentation**: Document analysis using provided template structure
6. **Recommendation Generation**: Provide actionable recommendations
