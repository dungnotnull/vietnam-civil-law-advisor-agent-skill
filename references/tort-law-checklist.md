# Tort and Civil Liability Checklist

Comprehensive issue-spotting and validation checklist for tort and civil liability matters under Vietnamese Civil Code 2015. Use this checklist when analyzing civil liability, damages, compensation, torts, or Article 584 matters.

## Checklist Overview

This checklist operationalizes the civil-law code-based syllogistic reasoning methodology for civil liability analysis. Each item represents a legal requirement derived from Civil Code 2015 articles, centered on the Article 584 framework.

**Core Framework:** Article 584, Civil Code 2015 establishes the grounds for civil liability requiring:
1. Illegal act
2. Damage occurred
3. Causal relationship between act and damage
4. Fault (intentional or negligent)

## Part 1: Civil Liability Framework

### 1.1 Grounds for Civil Liability (Article 584)

**Civil Code References:**
- Article 584: Grounds for civil liability
- Articles 513-530: General provisions on civil liability

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Illegal Act**: Is there an illegal act?
  - Act violates law (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 1)
  - Act violates social morality (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 1)
  - Omission violates legal duty (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 1)
  - Act constitutes violation of rights (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 1)

- [ ] **Damage Occurred**: Has damage occurred?
  - Property damage exists (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 2)
  - Personal injury exists (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 2)
  - Mental harm exists (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 2)
  - Damage is actual and proven (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 2)

- [ ] **Causal Relationship**: Is there causal relationship?
  - Direct causation established (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 3)
  - Act is proximate cause of damage (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 3)
  - No superseding cause breaks chain (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 3)
  - Causal link is proven (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 3)

- [ ] **Fault**: Is fault present?
  - Intentional conduct (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 4)
  - Negligent conduct (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 4)
  - Fault in act or omission (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 4)
  - Fault proven by evidence (CC2015, Art. 584, para. 4)

**Common Issues:**
- Disputed illegality of act
- Unclear damage proof
- Complex causation chains
- Fault attribution disputes

### 1.2 Types of Civil Liability

**Civil Code References:**
- Articles 514-518: Types of civil liability

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Contractual Liability**: Is liability contractual?
  - Breach of contract established (CC2015, Art. 516)
  - Contract terms govern liability (CC2015, Art. 516)
  - Damage results from breach (CC2015, Art. 516)

- [ ] **Non-Contractual Liability**: Is liability non-contractual?
  - Tort liability applies (CC2015, Art. 517)
  - No contract relationship exists (CC2015, Art. 517)
  - General tort principles apply (CC2015, Art. 517)

- [ ] **Strict Liability**: Does strict liability apply?
  - Dangerous activity involved (CC2015, Art. 518)
  - Environmental damage involved (CC2015, Art. 518)
  - Product liability involved (CC2015, Art. 518)
  - Fault not required (CC2015, Art. 518)

- [ ] **Fault-Based Liability**: Is liability fault-based?
  - Fault must be proven (CC2015, Art. 519)
  - Negligence standard applies (CC2015, Art. 519)
  - Intent standard applies (CC2015, Art. 519)

**Common Issues:**
- Liability type classification disputes
- Strict liability applicability disputes
- Fault proof difficulties
- Mixed liability scenarios

## Part 4: Defenses and Exemptions

### 4.1 Defenses to Civil Liability

**Civil Code References:**
- Articles 527-530: Exemption from and reduction of liability

**Checklist Items:**
- [ ] **Force Majeure**: Is force majeure available?
  - Event beyond control (CC2015, Art. 527)
  - Event could not be prevented (CC2015, Art. 527)
  - Event is sole cause of damage (CC2015, Art. 527)

- [ ] **Victim Fault**: Is victim fault a defense?
  - Victim contributed to damage (CC2015, Art. 528)
  - Comparative fault applies (CC2015, Art. 528)
  - Reduction of liability proportional (CC2015, Art. 528)

- [ ] **Act of Third Party**: Is third party act a defense?
  - Third party caused damage (CC2015, Art. 529)
  - Third party act broke causal chain (CC2015, Art. 529)
  - Defendant not responsible for third party (CC2015, Art. 529)

- [ ] **Consent**: Is consent a defense?
  - Victim consented to act (CC2015, Art. 530)
  - Consent valid and informed (CC2015, Art. 530)
  - Consent not contrary to law (CC2015, Art. 530)

**Common Issues:**
- Force majeure event disputes
- Victim fault allocation disputes
- Third party responsibility disputes
- Consent validity disputes
