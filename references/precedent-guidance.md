# Precedent Checking and Guidance

Comprehensive guide for checking, applying, and citing Supreme People's Court precedent and guidance in Vietnamese civil law analysis. This guide operationalizes precedent awareness within the civil-law tradition where code is primary but precedent provides important interpretive guidance.

## Role of Precedent in Vietnamese Civil Law

### Civil-Law Context

Vietnamese law follows the civil-law tradition where:
- **Code is primary**: Civil Code 2015 is the primary source of law
- **Precedent is supplementary**: Court guidance provides interpretation, not binding precedent
- **No stare decisis**: Courts are not strictly bound by prior decisions
- **Guidance value**: Supreme Court decisions provide important interpretive guidance

### Supreme People's Court Authority

The Supreme People's Court (SPC) issues:
- **Precedents (Án lệ)**: Selected cases with binding authority on specific points
- **Resolutions (Nghị quyết)**: Interpretive guidance on legal issues
- **Judgments (Bản án)**: Illustrative case decisions
- **Circulars (Thông tư)**: Implementation guidance

**Authority Levels:**
1. **Precedents**: Binding on lower courts for specific legal points
2. **Resolutions**: Strong interpretive guidance, widely followed
3. **Judgments**: Illustrative, persuasive but not binding
4. **Circulars**: Administrative guidance, not legal interpretation

## Precedent Checking Procedure

### Step 1: Identify Legal Issue

Before checking precedent, clearly identify the legal issue:

```
Legal Issue Format:
"Whether [specific legal question] under [relevant law articles]"

Example: "Whether a liquidated damages clause exceeding actual damage is enforceable under Article 363, Civil Code 2015"
```

### Step 2: Search for Relevant Precedent

Search systematically for relevant precedent:

**Search Sources (in priority order):**
1. **Official Precedents Database**: Supreme Court official precedents
2. **SPC Resolutions**: Resolutions on specific legal issues
3. **Published Judgments**: Selected published judgments
4. **Legal Commentaries**: Scholarly analysis of case law

**Search Terms:**
- Relevant Civil Code article numbers
- Key legal concepts (e.g., "liquidated damages," "land use rights")
- Specific legal questions
- Relevant legal domain (contract, property, family, tort)

### Step 3: Analyze Precedent Relevance

Analyze whether precedent is relevant to the current issue:

**Relevance Checklist:**
- [ ] Same legal question addressed
- [ ] Similar factual circumstances
- [ ] Same legal provisions involved
- [ ] Reasoning is applicable to current facts
- [ ] Precedent is current (not overruled)
- [ ] Precedent is from competent court

**Relevance Assessment:**

Highly Relevant:
- Same legal question, similar facts, current precedent

Moderately Relevant:
- Same legal question, different facts, current precedent

Low Relevance:
- Different legal question, different facts, or outdated precedent

Not Relevant:
- Different legal question or overruled precedent

### Step 4: Apply Precedent to Analysis

Apply relevant precedent to legal analysis:

**Application Format:**
```
Precedent Application:
1. Identify precedent: [Citation and brief description]
2. Explain relevance: [Why precedent applies to current issue]
3. Apply reasoning: [How precedent reasoning applies to current facts]
4. Derive conclusion: [Conclusion based on precedent application]
```

### Step 5: Document Precedent in Analysis

Document precedent consideration in analysis output:

**Documentation Format:**
```markdown
## Precedent Considerations

### Relevant Supreme Court Precedent
**Precedent**: [Precedent name and citation]
**Issue**: [Legal issue addressed by precedent]
**Holding**: [Court's holding on the issue]
**Relevance**: [How precedent applies to current analysis]

### Precedent Application
[Application of precedent reasoning to current facts]

### Gaps in Precedent
[Note if no relevant precedent exists and implications]
```

## Integration with Legal Analysis

### Contract Law Precedent Areas

**Key Areas Where Precedent is Important:**

1. **Contract Validity**
   - Interpretation of Article 117 (validity conditions)
   - Application of form requirements
   - Illegal purpose determination

2. **Breach and Remedies**
   - Application of liquidated damages (Article 363)
   - Calculation of damages (Article 360)
   - Specific performance availability (Article 365)

3. **Special Contract Types**
   - Sale and purchase disputes
   - Lease contract interpretation
   - Loan contract enforcement

**Precedent Citation in Contract Analysis:**
```markdown
### Precedent Considerations

**Liquidated Damages Precedent**: Precedent No. 04/2016/AL
- **Issue**: Enforceability of liquidated damages exceeding actual damage
- **Holding**: Liquidated damages may be reduced if they substantially exceed actual damage and constitute unjust enrichment
- **Application**: Current contract specifies 20% liquidated damages; actual damage estimated at 12%; precedent suggests reduction may be appropriate
- **Conclusion**: Clause may be reduced to approximately 12-15% under precedent guidance
```

### Property Law Precedent Areas

**Key Areas Where Precedent is Important:**

1. **Ownership and Land Use Rights**
   - Proof of ownership requirements
   - Land use right certificate validity
   - Foreign entity land rights

2. **Property Transactions**
   - Transfer formalities and registration
   - Mortgage enforcement procedures
   - Co-ownership partition disputes

3. **Property Disputes**
   - Boundary dispute resolution
   - Ownership dispute resolution
   - Recovery of property

**Precedent Citation in Property Analysis:**
```markdown
### Precedent Considerations

**Land Use Rights Precedent**: Precedent No. 12/2017/AL
- **Issue**: Validity of land use right transfer without registration
- **Holding**: Transfer of land use rights is not valid until registration completed; unregistered transfer creates no enforceable rights
- **Application**: Current transfer completed with written contract but registration pending; precedent indicates transfer not yet valid
- **Conclusion**: Parties must complete registration for transfer to be valid
```

### Family Law Precedent Areas

**Key Areas Where Precedent is Important:**

1. **Marriage and Divorce**
   - Grounds for divorce determination
   - Property division principles
   - Child custody considerations

2. **Inheritance**
   - Will validity requirements
   - Heir identification and rights
   - Estate division principles

**Precedent Citation in Family Law Analysis:**
```markdown
### Precedent Considerations

**Divorce Property Division Precedent**: Precedent No. 18/2018/AL
- **Issue**: Property division when contributions are unequal
- **Holding**: Courts may deviate from equal division when contributions to property acquisition are substantially unequal, considering both financial and non-financial contributions
- **Application**: Husband contributed 80% of purchase price, wife managed household and children; precedent supports unequal division recognizing both contributions
- **Conclusion**: Division may be 60-40 rather than 50-50, recognizing both financial and non-financial contributions
```

### Tort Liability Precedent Areas

**Key Areas Where Precedent is Important:**

1. **Civil Liability Framework**
   - Application of Article 584 elements
   - Causation analysis standards
   - Fault determination criteria

2. **Damages and Compensation**
   - Damage calculation methods
   - Non-economic damages assessment
   - Contributory fault application

**Precedent Citation in Tort Analysis:**
```markdown
### Precedent Considerations

**Civil Liability Precedent**: Precedent No. 25/2019/AL
- **Issue**: Application of Article 584 when multiple causes contribute to damage
- **Holding**: When multiple causes contribute to damage, liability is apportioned based on relative contribution; concurrent causation may reduce but not eliminate liability
- **Application**: Defendant's act contributed 60% to damage; victim's act contributed 40%; precedent supports apportionment of liability
- **Conclusion**: Defendant liable for 60% of total damages
```

## Precedent Citation Format

### Formal Citation Format

**Supreme Court Precedents:**
```
Precedent No. [Number]/[Year]/AL
Example: Precedent No. 04/2016/AL
```

**SPC Resolutions:**
```
Resolution No. [Number]/[Year]/NQ-HĐTP
Example: Resolution No. 03/2012/NQ-HĐTP
```

**Published Judgments:**
```
Judgment No. [Number]/[Year]/KT
Example: Judgment No. 12/2017/KT
```

### In-Text Citation

**Formal Citation:**
```
According to Precedent No. 04/2016/AL on liquidated damages...
```

**Parenthetical Citation:**
```
Liquidated damages may be reduced if substantially exceeding actual damage (Prec. No. 04/2016/AL).
```

**Short Citation:**
```
The 2016 liquidated damages precedent requires...
```

## Precedent Gaps and Limitations

### When No Precedent Exists

**Documenting Absence of Precedent:**
```markdown
### Gaps in Precedent
No directly applicable precedent was found addressing [specific legal issue] under [relevant law articles]. This absence means:

1. **Analysis relies on statutory interpretation**: Primary reliance on Civil Code 2015 text
2. **Predictive uncertainty**: Outcomes may be less predictable
3. **Potential for precedent creation**: This case may establish precedent if appealed to Supreme Court
4. **Need for careful analysis**: Extra care in legal reasoning given lack of guidance

**Implications**: Recommend [specific recommendation based on uncertainty]
```

### Precedent Limitations

**Common Limitations:**

1. **Factual Differences**: Precedent facts differ from current case
   - Acknowledge differences
   - Explain if differences are material
   - Apply reasoning by analogy if appropriate

2. **Procedural Differences**: Different procedural postures
   - Consider if procedural posture affects applicability
   - Apply substantive reasoning if appropriate

3. **Outdated Precedent**: Precedent may be outdated
   - Check if subsequent laws or precedents have addressed issue
   - Apply with caution if outdated

4. **Conflicting Precedent**: Multiple precedents may conflict
   - Identify conflict
   - Determine which precedent is more applicable
   - Acknowledge uncertainty if conflict cannot be resolved

## Precedent in IRAC Analysis

### Integrating Precedent into IRAC

**Issue**: State legal question
**Rule**: Cite Civil Code articles AND relevant precedent
**Application**: Apply both statutory law and precedent reasoning
**Conclusion**: Derive conclusion based on both sources

**Example:**
```
**Issue**: Whether liquidated damages clause exceeding actual damage is enforceable.

**Rule**: Article 363, Civil Code 2015 provides that liquidated damages may be agreed upon but courts may reduce excessive damages. Precedent No. 04/2016/AL holds that liquidated damages substantially exceeding actual damage may be reduced to prevent unjust enrichment.

**Application**: The contract specifies 20% liquidated damages. Actual damage from breach is estimated at 12% of contract value. Following Precedent No. 04/2016/AL, the 20% clause substantially exceeds actual damage and may constitute unjust enrichment.

**Conclusion**: The liquidated damages clause may be reduced to approximately 12-15% under Article 363 and Precedent No. 04/2016/AL.
```

## Practical Guidelines

### When Precedent Checking is Essential

**Essential Precedent Checking:**
- Novel legal questions
- Unclear statutory interpretation
- Complex fact patterns
- Significant financial stakes
- Potential for appeal

**Standard Precedent Checking:**
- Routine legal questions
- Clear statutory provisions
- Simple fact patterns
- Lower stakes matters

### Precedent Quality Assessment

**High-Quality Precedent:**
- Recent Supreme Court precedent
- Directly on point
- Well-reasoned opinion
- Widely cited and followed

**Moderate-Quality Precedent:**
- Older Supreme Court precedent
- Somewhat on point
- Adequate reasoning
- Some citation history

**Low-Quality Precedent:**
- Lower court decision
- Indirectly on point
- Limited reasoning
- Little citation history

### Precedent Application Best Practices

**Do:**
- Check for current precedent
- Apply relevant precedent systematically
- Explain precedent reasoning
- Acknowledge when no precedent exists
- Distinguish non-applicable precedent
- Update precedent checking regularly

**Don't:**
- Assume no precedent exists without checking
- Apply outdated precedent
- Overstate precedent authority
- Ignore contradictory precedent
- Apply precedent without explanation
- Rely solely on foreign precedent

## Conclusion

Precedent provides valuable interpretive guidance within Vietnamese civil-law tradition. While code is primary, Supreme Court precedent illuminates statutory interpretation and application. Systematic precedent checking ensures comprehensive legal analysis and helps predict likely judicial outcomes.

**Key Principles:**
1. Code is primary; precedent is supplementary
2. Precedent provides interpretive guidance, not binding rules
3. Apply precedent systematically and explain its relevance
4. Acknowledge when no precedent exists
5. Update precedent research regularly
6. Understand precedent's place within civil-law methodology

By integrating precedent checking into legal analysis, practitioners provide more comprehensive and predictive advice while respecting the civil-law tradition's code-centered approach.
